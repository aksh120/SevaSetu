# NGO Registration: Real-World Journey and SevaSetu Product Assessment

**Status: Prototype research document**

## Important prototype boundary

SevaSetu is a product prototype and navigation aid. It is not a government department, registrar, tax authority, ministry, bank, or legal representative. Any document upload, review, submission, status, or approval shown in the product is simulated for demonstration. SevaSetu cannot register an NGO, issue a certificate, grant tax exemption, grant FCRA permission, approve CSR eligibility, or make an organisation official.

In a production version, every official application would still be submitted to the relevant authority and the authority alone would decide the outcome.

## Executive summary

Without a guided solution, a first-time NGO founder has to work out three different problems at the same time:

1. Choose the correct legal form: Trust, Society, or Section 8 Company.
2. Complete several dependent registrations and filings across different systems.
3. Maintain evidence, respond to queries, and track deadlines after submission.

The journey is not one universal “NGO registration” form. The founder normally creates a legal entity first, then obtains an organisation PAN and a bank account, then applies for tax-related registrations and NGO Darpan. CSR-1 and FCRA are conditional based on funding plans and eligibility. The exact authority, documents, fees, forms, signing method, and in-person requirements vary by structure and state.

SevaSetu makes the journey easier by turning a fragmented research problem into a personalised sequence, explaining why each step exists, carrying a reusable checklist, showing dependencies, translating notices into plain language, and giving the founder one progress view. It does not replace the official portals or professional/legal advice.

## What people currently have to do without SevaSetu

### 1. Decide what “registration” means

The founder must first understand that “NGO” is a broad description, not usually a single legal entity type. The practical choices are:

- **Trust:** usually created through a trust deed and registered under the applicable state process. The relevant office may be a Sub-Registrar or state public trust authority, depending on the state and purpose.
- **Society:** usually requires a founding group and governing documents. The authority is generally the state Registrar of Societies or the state’s online service system.
- **Section 8 Company:** incorporated through the Ministry of Corporate Affairs using the company incorporation workflow, including name and incorporation forms, electronic signatures, and director-related requirements.

The founder has to compare governance, number of founders, operating geography, compliance load, donor expectations, and future funding plans before filing. A wrong choice can create restructuring work later.

### 2. Visit or use the relevant systems and offices

The exact route depends on the selected structure and the state. A founder may need to use or contact:

| Need | Typical portal or office | What the founder does there |
|---|---|---|
| Trust registration | State public trust authority, Sub-Registrar, or state registration portal | Prepare and execute the trust deed, pay applicable charges, submit identity/address evidence, and obtain the registered deed or certificate. |
| Society registration | State Registrar of Societies or state citizen-services portal | Submit memorandum, rules/bylaws, member details, address proof, declarations, and respond to scrutiny or resubmission. |
| Section 8 Company | Ministry of Corporate Affairs / MCA portal and Central Registration Centre | Check or reserve a name, prepare the incorporation application and constitutional documents, attach declarations, use DSCs where required, and respond to MCA queries. |
| Organisation PAN and related tax services | Income Tax e-Filing / PAN service route | Apply for the entity’s PAN after legal formation and use the correct entity details. TAN may also be relevant depending on the organisation’s obligations. |
| Organisation bank account | Bank branch or bank’s account-opening process | Provide the registration proof, PAN, governing-body resolution, KYC, authorised signatory details, and beneficial/control information requested by the bank. |
| Tax exemption and donor benefit applications | Income Tax e-Filing portal | Apply using the currently applicable forms and provisions for charitable registration and donor-related approval. Names, form numbers, and requirements can change, so the current portal instructions must be checked. |
| NGO directory / government-facing profile | NGO Darpan portal operated by NITI Aayog | Create the NGO profile, verify the authorised person, provide entity and governing-body information, and obtain or maintain the Darpan ID where required. |
| CSR eligibility filing | MCA portal | Complete the applicable CSR-1 process after checking the current eligibility, tax-registration, certificate, and signing requirements. |
| Foreign contribution | FCRA portal administered by the Ministry of Home Affairs | Apply for registration or prior permission only when eligible, disclose office-bearers and activities, use the prescribed bank route, and comply with ongoing reporting rules. |
| Professional help or clarification | Chartered accountant, company secretary, advocate, tax practitioner, or local filing agent | Resolve structure, drafting, tax, signing, resubmission, accounting, and compliance questions. |

This is a planning map, not a substitute for current government instructions. State processes differ materially, and the founder must verify the official route for the state and legal form selected.

## Main real-world challenges

### A. The process is fragmented

There is no single case file shared across all authorities. The same facts are repeatedly entered into different portals, PDFs, declarations, and office workflows. A name, address, objective, trustee/member list, or date mismatch can trigger a query.

### B. The dependency chain is easy to miss

The legal structure generally comes before the organisation PAN. PAN and formation records are then used for the bank account and later tax or directory applications. Darpan may be needed for later funding or FCRA-related work. CSR-1 depends on the organisation having the relevant eligibility and documents. FCRA is not a normal first-day registration: regular registration requires an operating track record, while prior permission is a different route for a defined contribution or project.

### C. Founders cannot easily choose the right structure

A founder may know the social mission but not the consequences of choosing a Trust, Society, or Section 8 Company. Advice found online is often written for one structure, one state, or one funding goal and may not explain trade-offs.

### D. Document preparation is repetitive and error-prone

Typical evidence can include address proof, identity proof, photographs, trust deed or memorandum, rules and bylaws, governing-body details, resolutions, declarations, PAN, bank evidence, activity descriptions, audited accounts, and prior filings. Requirements differ by authority. Scans may be unclear, signatures may be missing, or the wrong file may be attached.

### E. Legal language hides the actual action

Notices commonly use formal terms such as deficiency, resubmission, clarification, authorised signatory, object clause, certified copy, or compliance. A founder may understand that something is wrong but still not know what to correct, which file to replace, or the deadline.

### F. Status tracking is scattered

The founder may have separate login credentials, acknowledgement numbers, email threads, SMS messages, paper receipts, and office conversations. There is often no single view of what is submitted, what is pending, what was queried, and what comes next.

### G. Offline work may still be necessary

Even when an application starts online, the founder may need notarisation, stamp paper, deed execution, certified copies, bank KYC, DSC setup, physical signatures, document scanning, or an office visit. Which of these applies depends on the structure, state, authority, and current rules.

### H. Deadlines and compliance continue after formation

Registration is the beginning of the organisation’s administrative work. The NGO may later need accounting, audit, income-tax filings, annual returns, renewal or conversion from provisional status, donor reporting, CSR records, FCRA returns, board/member records, and changes to registered information.

### I. Language and digital access create exclusion

Many founders work primarily in Hindi or another Indian language, use a phone instead of a desktop, have limited scanning facilities, or rely on an agent. A portal that assumes high legal and digital literacy creates a practical barrier even when the filing is technically online.

### J. Rules and form names change

Tax provisions, portal workflows, fees, document requirements, and eligibility rules can change. Fixed numbers or old screenshots can cause harm when treated as current law. A useful product must show the source date and send users to the current official instructions before filing.

## How SevaSetu makes the journey easier

| Existing problem | SevaSetu prototype response | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Founder does not know where to start | Four-question intake captures structure status, funding plans, and organisation age. | It recommends a planning sequence; it does not choose or legally establish the entity. |
| Every NGO sees the same generic list | Roadmap adds CSR-1 or FCRA conditionally based on funding answers. | Eligibility must still be confirmed with the authority or qualified professional. |
| Dependencies are hidden | Steps appear in order: structure, PAN, tax registrations, Darpan, then conditional funding tracks. | The exact order can vary by state, entity type, and current rules. |

| Users assume the product has submitted a filing | Mocked badges identify upload, submission, and status touchpoints. | This disclosure must remain prominent everywhere a simulated outcome appears. |

## What the current mock demonstrates

The working prototype currently demonstrates:

- An intake flow for the organisation name, legal structure status, funding plans, and organisation age.
- A personalised roadmap with base steps and conditional CSR-1/FCRA steps.
- A new-organisation FCRA callout explaining that a track record may be required.
- Guided modules with document checklists and simulated uploads.
- Simulated submission and status progression.
- A local dashboard with progress and next action.
- A notice translator with a pre-loaded example and a paste path.
- English and Hindi interface content.
- An explicit mocked badge and product disclosure for simulated government touchpoints.
- A print/export-style roadmap suitable for carrying to a qualified professional or official portal workflow.

## What the current mock does not do

It does not:

- Create a Trust, Society, or Section 8 Company.
- Submit a form to MCA, a state registrar, Income Tax, NGO Darpan, MCA CSR systems, MHA/FCRA, or a bank.
- Verify identity, PAN, Aadhaar, DSC, bank information, or uploaded documents.
- Store or validate an official document record.
- Contact an office or obtain an acknowledgement number.
- Issue a registration certificate, Darpan ID, tax approval, CSR eligibility, FCRA permission, or bank account.
- Guarantee that a checklist is complete or that an application will be accepted.
- Make the NGO “official” or represent an authority’s approval.

## Recommended production safeguards

Before connecting any real filing workflow, the product should add:

1. Official portal links beside each step, with the authority and last-checked date.
2. A state-and-structure decision layer before showing document requirements.
3. Versioned checklists so users can see which rule or portal instruction they reflect.
4. A clear separation between “prepared,” “ready to file,” “submitted by user,” and “officially accepted.”
5. User-controlled storage with encryption, retention limits, deletion, and consent before handling identity or financial documents.
6. Human review or referral for structure choice, tax interpretation, foreign funding, and unusual notices.
7. No simulated approval language outside demo mode. A real status must be sourced from an official acknowledgement or authorised integration.
8. An escalation path when an authority rejects, queries, or changes a form.
9. Accessibility and language testing with actual founders, including low-bandwidth and phone-only scenarios.
10. A visible disclaimer on every page and every generated/exported roadmap.
